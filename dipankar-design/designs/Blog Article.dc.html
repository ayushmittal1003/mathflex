Your 100-day JEE Main plan, chapter by chapter

Which chapters to finish first, when to start mocks and how to fit revision around school.

KKaran Mittal · B.Tech, IIT Delhi
2 Oct 2026 · 9 min read

A hundred days sounds like a lot until you list the chapters. JEE Main maths has 35 of them across Class 11 and 12, and most students reach the last three months with half of them still shaky. This plan is the one I give students on our 1:1 calls. It works if you follow it roughly, not perfectly.

Start with what carries the most marks

Not every chapter is worth the same. Five chapters together make up roughly a quarter of the maths paper, so they come first.

The plan in four phases

Day 1–35
Finish the high-weightage chapters
One chapter every 6–7 days. Watch the parts, solve the DPP the same day and do the PYQs on the weekend.
Day 36–65
Cover the rest, lightly
Smaller chapters like Statistics, Sets and LPP take 2–3 days each. Aim for the PYQs, not every textbook exercise.
Day 66–85
Mocks and fixing gaps
Two full mocks a week. Spend as long reviewing a mock as you spent writing it.
Day 86–100
Revise, don’t learn
Only formula sheets, mind maps and your own mistake log. No new chapters in the last two weeks.
Karan's tip

If a chapter isn't clicking after two days, move on and come back in phase 3. Being stuck for a week costs more marks than skipping.

What a normal day looks like

  • 40 minutes of video, one part at a time, before school or right after.
  • 30 minutes on that part’s DPP the same evening, while it’s fresh.
  • 20 minutes on yesterday’s mistakes, written into a single notebook.
  • Sundays for PYQs and one timed section test.

When to start mock tests

Start full mocks in phase 3, not before. Earlier than that, a mock mostly tells you which chapters you haven’t done yet, which you already know. Until then, do chapter-wise section tests every Sunday.

After each mock, sort every wrong answer into one of three buckets: didn’t know the concept, knew it but made a slip, or ran out of time. The first bucket goes back into your chapter list. The other two go into your mistake log.

If you’re already behind

Cut phase 2 short. Pick the high-weightage chapters you haven’t done, skip the rest for now and protect the last two weeks for revision. A smaller syllabus you know well scores more than a full syllabus you half-know.
